Research brief · 7 September 2026 · pre-build

Pixel Dividend

A 32×32 board on Robinhood Chain. 1,024 tiles, one NFT each. Every tile is staked to a real tokenized stock, and the treasury's yield is paid out per tile.

This document is the groundwork: which contract addresses are genuine and how that was proven, where the yield actually comes from, how the game is played, and the 1-bit system the whole thing is drawn in.

The board

Example board state, not live data. Hover or arrow-key across a tile to inspect it. Fill density is the tile's yield weight; white outlines mark territory borders.

01

What is actually verified

Nothing below was copied from a token list or an article.

Every address in this brief was read straight off Robinhood Chain on 7 September 2026, around block 0x35e6ed7. Three checks, in order:

  1. eth_getCodeEach genuine stock token is a 283-byte ERC-1967 beacon proxy. All fifty are byte-identical — same factory, same deployment.
  2. eth_getStorageAt → beacon slotThe beacon pointer must equal 0xe10b6f6b…62151b00. This is the check that carries the weight; the rest is corroboration.
  3. symbol() and decimals()Symbol matches the ticker, decimals is 18. This is where the registry was caught being wrong once — see below.

Never verify by name

A token called TheGreenHood trades on this chain under the symbol HOOD with 1,137 holders. It passes any name or symbol check a person would do by eye. Its beacon slot is empty, so it fails check 2 instantly.

Five separate tokens use the symbol USDG. Four are 44-byte minimal-proxy clones with a suspiciously round 1,000,000,000 supply and 18 decimals. The real Global Dollar has 6 decimals — that alone separates it.

The explorer is not a source

Blockscout labels SK hynix as SKYHY. The contract's own symbol() returns SKHY. The first draft of the registry shipped the explorer's label; the verification script rejected it and the build failed, which is exactly what it is for.

It is also the only genuine token not named “… • Robinhood Token”, which is why the naming convention cannot be a rule.

02

Where the yield comes from

This is the part that makes the concept work, and it is not obvious.

A Robinhood stock token does not pay a dividend into your wallet. Instead the contract carries a uiMultiplier(), and the token's value is underlying price × multiplier. When a dividend is reinvested or a split happens, Robinhood calls updateMultiplier() and the number moves. Chainlink's feed reads that same multiplier to publish a total-return price.

So yield accrues inside the token itself. A treasury that simply holds the token captures it — no claim step, no distribution contract on Robinhood's side, nothing to integrate. That is what makes “pixels pay real yield” mechanically honest rather than a slogan.

Read live on 7 September 2026, ten weeks after mainnet. Most tickers still sit at exactly 1.0 — they have not paid anything yet:

SGOV is the engine, the famous names are the draw

SGOV is a 0–3 month Treasury ETF. Its multiplier moved 0.510% in 68 days — roughly a 2.8% observed run-rate if it continues at that pace. It is the only instrument here throwing off yield on a predictable schedule, and it should be the treasury's base layer.

NVDA, TSLA, GME and SPCX are all sitting at 1.000000. They contribute nothing as yield — their draw is price exposure and name recognition. Design the game so the base reward is real and the famous tickers are the bet, not the other way round. That run-rate is an observation over ten weeks, not a promise, and it must never be printed in the UI as an APY.

04

How the game is played

A board game needs a decision. This is the decision.

Holding an NFT is not a game. The game is that a tile has to be positioned, and the position can be wrong. Each round every tile is staked to one ticker, and what a tile earns depends on the ticker's performance and on who its neighbours are.

  1. ClaimMint one of 1,024 tiles. Each is a fixed coordinate on the board — A1 through AF32. Position is permanent; the tile is the deed to a square.
  2. StakePoint the tile at one of the sixteen Phase 1 tickers. This is the move, and it can be changed once per round.
  3. TerritoryOrthogonally adjacent tiles staked to the same ticker form a territory. A territory of n tiles earns a bonus weight that scales with n, so coordinating with your neighbours beats optimising alone. This is the whole social layer: block trades, alliances, and squeezing someone out of a corner.
  4. SettleAt round close the Chainlink feeds are read. Treasury yield for the round is split across tiles by weight: ticker performance × territory bonus. Losing tickers still earn the SGOV floor — a bad call costs upside, not principal.
  5. Compound or claimTake the payout in the staked stock token, or leave it in the treasury and let the multiplier keep working. Claiming is a transfer; both paths stay on-chain.

Why a contract can hold these tokens at all

This was worth checking before designing anything. A transfer was simulated from a real holder to both an EOA and a contract address — both returned success, so there is no allowlist and no transfer restriction. The implementation exposes isBlocked(address), pause() and adminBurn(), but tokenPaused() and oraclePaused() read false across every ticker checked. It also implements permit() (ERC-2612), so staking a tile can be a single signature with no separate approval transaction.

05

The 1-bit system

Black and white, taken literally rather than as a mood.

A monochrome brief usually turns into “dark UI with one accent colour”. This one takes the constraint at face value: there is no accent hue anywhere. Emphasis is inversion — a white block with black type. Quantity is dither density. Status is fill pattern: solid means verified, hatched means suspect. On a board made of pixels that is the native vocabulary, and it means the system survives being printed, screenshotted, or rendered into an NFT image at 32×32.

Board encoding

  • Density — Bayer 4×4 dither, six levels. Encodes the tile's yield weight.
  • Outline — 1px white where a tile's neighbour holds a different ticker. Draws territories.
  • Inversion — the selected tile. One selection at a time, so it never competes.
  • Empty — unminted tiles stay black with only the grid hairline.

Rules that hold it together

  • image-rendering: pixelated everywhere; no anti-aliased board.
  • Canvas drawn at devicePixelRatio, logical grid 32×32 at 18px cells.
  • Sixteen tickers is well past what dither patterns can distinguish — identity comes from the readout, never from pattern alone.
  • Focus states visible; the board is arrow-key navigable, not mouse-only.

Build

React + Vite + TypeScript, Reown for the wallet.

Front end

  • Vite + React 19 + TypeScript
  • @reown/appkit + @reown/appkit-adapter-wagmi
  • wagmi + viem + TanStack Query
  • Board on <canvas>, not 1,024 DOM nodes
  • Multicall3 at 0xcA11bde0…3976CA11 for batched tile reads

Contracts

  • ERC-721, supply hard-capped at 1,024
  • Treasury holding the Phase 1 basket
  • Round settlement reading Chainlink latestRoundData()
  • Pull-payment claims — never loop 1,024 transfers
  • Foundry; deploy to chainId 46630 first

07

What could go wrong

Worth deciding on deliberately rather than discovering later.

Issuer control is real

The token implementation includes pause(), adminBurn(address,uint256) and isBlocked(address), and the beacon is upgradeable — one call changes all fifty tokens at once. Nothing is paused or blocked today, but the treasury's assets sit behind a switch someone else owns. Make round settlement degrade safely if a ticker is paused mid-round, and hold the SGOV floor in something you can still pay out.

These are not shares

Stock tokens give economic exposure, issued by Robinhood Assets (Jersey) Limited — not legal ownership, no voting. Paying yield derived from securities-tracking assets to NFT holders sits squarely in securities territory in most jurisdictions, and availability already varies by country. Get this in front of a lawyer before the mint, not after, and keep every surface saying pass-through rather than APY or guaranteed.

Open decisions

  • Mint price and currency. USDG is the chain's settlement asset and the obvious choice, but it pulls a 6-decimal token into an 18-decimal codebase — handle that conversion in one place or it will bite.
  • Round length. A week gives price moves room to matter and keeps settlement gas low; a day makes it a game people check daily. This changes the whole feel.
  • Whether the treasury is seeded from mint proceeds or funded up front so round one already pays.
  • Territory bonus curve. Linear in n is easy to reason about; anything steeper concentrates the board into a few whales by round five.
